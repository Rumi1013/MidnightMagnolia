Who this is for

If you've been calling it "just tired,"

You may have been doing the surface work for a while now. The reading. The podcasts. The therapy intake forms. Maybe even the bigger journals.

And still — there is something underneath that has not been named, let alone set down. The shame you can't quite locate. The feeling beneath the feeling. The thing you keep turning away from because you don't know what to do once you face it.

You don't need another framework to keep up with. You need a first honest pass — a structured, gentle, finite ritual that asks the right questions and then closes its own door behind you.

The Shadow Work Starter Kit is exactly that. Thirty prompts. Three arcs. One month, or one season, or however slowly you need to move.

It is anchored to the second root of the Five Roots Theory of Change: Burn It. The work of separating the experience that was yours from the stigma that was absorbed from outside. Burn the stigma. Keep the person.

The Structure

Three arcs. Thirty days.

The kit is structured the way the brain actually moves through this work — not the way self-help books pretend it does. You begin with ground. You examine a pattern. You return to the present with something gentler than you started with.

i.
Days 1–10

Foundation · Ground beneath you

The first ten days set the floor. What are you actually feeling — past the surface word? Where in your body does the feeling live? What did you absorb from outside that was never yours to begin with? The work of these prompts is to name without verdict.

Sample register: feelings wheel · body check-in · stigma inventory · what's mine vs. what's absorbed
ii.
Days 11–20

Pattern · What keeps repeating

Days eleven through twenty move into honest examination. Where is the loop? What story have you been telling about yourself that doesn't quite hold up under direct sunlight? What does this pattern give you, and what does it cost you? This arc is the most demanding. It is also the most freeing.

Sample register: cost-benefit · the four agreements · inherited vs. constructed · what would be true if the worst story wasn't
iii.
Days 21–30

Return · What you carry now

The final ten days are about what is yours to keep, what is yours to put down, and what tending you owe yourself once the burning is done. The arc closes with a return — not to who you were before, but to the version of you that did the work.

Sample register: what stays · what gets composted · daily best (which changes day to day) · the closing letter
Day 3 · Foundation

"The feeling beneath the feeling I called 'fine' this week was — and the version of me that needed to call it 'fine' was protecting what?"

Day 14 · Pattern

"This pattern is giving me ____ and costing me ____. The first time it was useful to me, I was approximately ___ years old."

Day 27 · Return

"One small thing I will tend tomorrow — not fix, not optimize, just tend — is ____. If 'my best' looks different tomorrow than it does today, both versions count."

Lineage

The root behind "Burn It."

Every Midnight Magnolia product is anchored to one of the Five Roots. The Shadow Work Starter Kit is anchored to the second — Burn It — which is informed by four sources, named once here and never extracted for content.

The Sources

Four traditions
held alongside each other.

Burn the Stigma — a recovery framework whose central instruction is the title itself: burn the stigma, keep the person. The work is not to destroy the self; it is to separate the experience from the shame absorbed about the experience.

MARA (Medication-Assisted Recovery Anonymous) — whose preamble names non-judgment as the code. The kit borrows MARA's posture of shame-free inventory without claiming the fellowship itself.

Recovery Dharma — whose First Noble Truth holds that suffering exists not as a verdict on the sufferer but as the condition that makes practice possible. The kit's tone is informed by this.

Hoodoo fire practice — a specific lineage technology from the Black Southern ancestral tradition that informs my work. It is named here once, in context, and not elaborated on. Specific practices belong to lineage; the kit borrows the logic of release through fire, never the practice itself.
